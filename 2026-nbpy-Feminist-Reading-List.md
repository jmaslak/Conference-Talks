# Feminist Reading List

## Where to Get These Books / Articles

Consider supporting your local bookstore, particularly if your bookstore is
working to challenge oppression (plenty are!). I've given links to [Fabulosa
Books](https://www.fabulosabooks.com/#/) (an amazing bookstore in the Castro
District of San Francisco) online ordering, but your purchases will make a big
difference to your local bookstore wherever that is.

In addition, most of these books are available at your local library. If your
local library doesn't have them, they can request it from another library for
you–all typically at no cost to you. This is one reason libraries are so
important–-not everyone works in tech with big salaries (heck, not everyone in
tech has a big salary), but this information is really important!

Many of these also are available as audiobooks, and you can also find lectures
and informative videos online on any of these topics!

## These Categories

Books could be categorized in a ton of places here, but I placed them in just
one. There are also things that are missing that are every bit as amazing. I
gathered books that changed my views, inspired, and called me to action. But
feminism is a huge field, and feminists, like others who fight oppression,
co-create theory & praxis. So you’ll find many books on all these topics, and
books I categorized in one area may flow into another, as they are in
conversation with each other.

## Limitations

Some of these resources are older, and theory has evolved since they were
created. For instance, I love Julia Serano's Whipping Girl, which is mentioned
below, but it doesn't engage with non-binary identity and uses some older
language. I believe we can still learn from books such as these even when they
are imperfect. Take what is awesome, leave what is not!

In addition, some of these books might discuss difficult and even triggering
topics, so it may be good to read the tables of contents or online reviews
prior to purchase. Some are explicitly sex-positive and frankly discuss and
celebrate sexuality, which may not be acceptable in some contexts.

## Introduction to Feminism

 * bell hooks
   * Feminism is for Everybody ([book](https://bookshop.org/p/books/feminism-is-for-everybody-passionate-politics-bell-hooks/6b4ecfbe759f1879?ean=9781138821620))
     * A relatively easy-to-read introduction, exactly what it says on the tin!

## Intersectionality

 * Kimberlé Crenshaw
   * Demarginalizing the Intersection of Race & Sex: A Black Feminist Critique of Antidiscrimination Doctrine, Feminist Theory and Antiracist Politics ([online](https://chicagounbound.uchicago.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1052&context=uclf))
     * The first use of "intersectionality" (1989). Written for a law journal.
   * Mapping the Margins: Intersectionality, Identity Politics, and Violence against Women of Color ([online](https://blogs.law.columbia.edu/critique1313/files/2020/02/1229039.pdf))
     * Her followup article on intersectionality in 1991.
 * The Combahee River Collective
   * A Feminist Statement ([online](https://americanstudies.yale.edu/sites/default/files/files/Keyword%20Coalition_Readings.pdf))
     * One of the most influential feminist writings, written in the 1970s in response to treatment of Black women by white feminists of the time not recognizing the "interlocking oppressions" Black women faced. Probably my (Joelle) favorite feminist writing ever–this is the writing that caused me to major in gender studies.

## Black Feminism
 * Patricia Hill Collins
   * Black Feminist Thought ([book](https://bookshop.org/p/books/black-feminist-thought-30th-anniversary-edition-knowledge-consciousness-and-the-politics-of-empowerment-patricia-hill-collins/49a0fcd5816ec45a?ean=9781032157832&next=t))
     * An academic text that examines Black feminism. Essential reading, particularly for white feminists!

## Fighting for Change

 * Audre Lorde
   * The Master’s Tools Will Never Dismantle the Master’s House ([online](https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/audre-lorde-the-master-s-tools-will-never-dismantle-the-master-s-house))
     * An essay against systems that tokenize individuals rather than fight the system’s own problems.

## Transfeminism / Transgender

 * Julia Serano
   * Whipping Girl: A Transsexual Woman on Sexism and the Scapegoating of Femininity ([book](https://bookshop.org/p/books/whipping-girl-a-transsexual-woman-on-sexism-and-the-scapegoating-of-femininity-julia-serano/4a5c8c6646a6caca?ean=9781541604520&next=t))
     * The canonical transfeminism book.
 * Jules Gill-Peterson ([book](https://bookshop.org/p/books/histories-of-the-transgender-child-jules-gill-peterson/c85d7e5a11dfb99c?ean=9781517904678&next=t))
   * Histories of the Transgender Child
     * Right now, trans kids are the target of hate and misinformation. This book talks about how they've always been with us, and how medicine has approached trans youth. It’s enlightening that what we think is new (for instance, hormone therapy for teens) is not.

## Doing Justice

 * Leah Lakshmi Piepzna-Samarashinha
   * Care Work: Dreaming Disability Justice ([book](https://bookshop.org/p/books/care-work-dreaming-disability-justice-leah-lakshmi-piepzna-samarasinha/64dfa473415265f1?ean=9781551527383&next=t))
     * How do you build resilience to do justice? Reading this book as a trans, autistic woman satisfied a thirst I didn't know I had.
